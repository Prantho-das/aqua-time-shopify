SECTION 3 — WHAT IS AN ATMOSPHERIC WATER GENERATOR?
H2: What Is an Atmospheric Water Generator?
An atmospheric water generator, or AWG, is a system designed to extract water vapor from ambient air and convert it into liquid water. Many systems accomplish this by drawing air into the machine, filtering the incoming air, cooling it to the dew point, collecting condensation, and then treating the collected water through additional filtration or purification processes.
Instead of transporting water from a distant source, atmospheric water generation moves part of the water-production process closer to the place where the water will actually be used.
The technology can range from compact systems designed for drinking water to larger systems capable of supporting commercial, industrial, institutional, and community applications.
EPA research describes condensation-based atmospheric water generation as a technology that pulls moisture from surrounding air, while noting that production depends heavily on available water vapor and temperature.

² Arsenic is a naturally occurring contaminant in Texas groundwater. Some wells, particularly in West Texas, have recorded levels above the EPA drinking water standard of 10 µg/L. Sources: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and U.S. Geological Survey (USGS).
H3: Nitrates
Why it matters: Elevated nitrate levels can pose serious health risks, particularly for infants and pregnant women.
Where it’s found: Higher nitrate concentrations are more common in rural and agricultural areas where fertilizers and animal waste can affect groundwater.
³ Nitrate contamination in rural Texas is often associated with agricultural runoff, fertilizer use, and livestock operations. According to the 2025 Water Stress Index by Salford & Co., agricultural activity represented 24% of active groundwater contamination cases in the state.
H3: Microplastics
Why it matters: Researchers are still studying the long-term health effects of microplastic exposure, including possible impacts on inflammation and hormone function.
Where it’s found: Microplastics have been detected in treated drinking water and may enter water supplies through environmental pollution, aging infrastructure, and other sources.
⁴ Microplastics are plastic particles smaller than 5 mm that have been found in drinking water systems in the United States and around the world. Research into their potential effects on human health is ongoing. Source: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).

SECTION 15 — FAQ
H2: Frequently Asked Questions About Air to Water Generators
H3: What is an atmospheric water generator?
An atmospheric water generator is a system that captures water vapor from ambient air and converts it into liquid water. Depending on the system and intended application, the collected water may then undergo additional filtration, purification, disinfection, mineralization, or storage.
H3: How does an air to water generator make water?
Most condensation-based systems draw humid air across a cooled surface. When the air reaches its dew point, water vapor condenses into liquid droplets that can be collected and treated.
H3: Does an atmospheric water generator need a water connection?
Atmospheric water generators obtain their source water from moisture in the air. Some systems can therefore generate water without a traditional incoming water supply, although installation requirements differ by system.
H3: How much water can an atmospheric water generator produce?
Production depends on the specific equipment as well as temperature, humidity, operating time, and other environmental conditions. Systems can range from smaller drinking-water units to much larger commercial systems.
H3: Do atmospheric water generators work in dry climates?
They can operate under a range of conditions, but available humidity strongly affects water production and efficiency. The specific operating envelope of the equipment should always be reviewed before installation.
H3: Is water made from air safe to drink?
That depends on the complete system and its treatment process—not condensation alone. Systems intended to produce drinking water should be evaluated according to their treatment technology, testing, certifications, maintenance requirements, and applicable water-quality standards.
H3: Can atmospheric water generators operate off-grid?
Some systems can be incorporated into off-grid configurations when appropriate power, installation, water treatment, and storage requirements are provided.
H3: What size air to water generator do I need?
System sizing should consider expected daily water demand, application, temperature, relative humidity, hours of operation, available electrical service, storage, and desired redundancy.
